Documentation

metering computes quantities — kWh, m³ and kW — for the German energy market. It performs no I/O, spawns no runtime, never reads the system clock, and never routes a metered quantity through a floating-point number.

These guides explain the parts that carry domain knowledge you cannot infer from the type signatures. For the full API, see docs.rs/metering.

Pages

  • Readings and intervals — Zählerstandsgang to Lastgang: differencing meter readings, reconstructing register wraps safely, and why the conversion never invents a value.
  • Validation and quality — The order-independent rule engine V01–V12, why the outlier test is a Hampel identifier, and the A/B/C/F grade layered over it.
  • Ersatzwertbildung — Substitute values under § 60 MsbG: the four methods, the calendar-aware grid, and why the audit trail records what ran rather than what was asked for.
  • Tariff registers — HT/NT and § 14a EnWG Modul 3 in one mechanism: ordered windows over months, day groups and minute bands, resolved in Europe/Berlin local time.
  • Gas conversion and units — m³ to kWh_Hs under MessEG and DVGW G 685, why the Betriebsvolumen matters, and unit normalisation that refuses to guess.
  • Power quality — EN 50160 is a statistical standard over a week of 10-minute means, not a per-interval threshold — and what that changes.
  • Virtual meters — § 42b EnWG Gemeinschaftliche Gebäudeversorgung: constant and proportional PV allocation, and the two caps that apply.
  • Regulatory basis — Every provision this library implements, quoted from the published text and dated — including the ones it deliberately does not claim.
  • Design constraints — Determinism, exact decimals, one canonical string per value, serde stability and exhaustive domain enums — the invariants the library holds to.
